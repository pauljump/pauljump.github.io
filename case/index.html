01

field work → product rail

Kit · New York OPWDD Self-Direction

Selected product cases

Turn a paper-and-phone workflow into an approval-readiness system.

This began as a passion project with a nonprofit I met through my board work with the Simons Foundation. In New York OPWDD Self-Direction, a family can have an approved budget and still spend weeks moving one request through a provider, care manager, support broker, and Fiscal Intermediary. The work was paper, email, phone calls, and one-off packets. I built the system that compiles that work before a family has to carry it.

What the field looked like

The request lived between institutions

A parent found a class or service, then became the integration layer. They had to determine the funding route, establish the actual offering and provider facts, get the right evidence, and learn the FI’s submission rules. Most of that work was repeated the next time another family asked for the same service.

The product decision

Compile the route before the request arrives

Kit turns policy, FI process, provider evidence, and the family’s private facts into a bounded readiness prediction: likely fundable, fundable with fixes, unlikely as described, or insufficient verified information. It produces the packet and next action behind that result, rather than a black-box score.

What the system actually is

Shared evidence stays reusable. Private case data and final decisions do not.

01 Statewide rail

One versioned decision and evidence contract per service branch determines the required packet and the route it belongs on.

02 FI adapter

The selected FI adds its exact form, channel, timing, pre-approval, vendor setup, and correction workflow without forking statewide policy.

03 Provider evidence graph

Organization, location, offering, price, payee, and reimbursement facts are source-linked, hashed, and reusable across family requests instead of rediscovered ad hoc.

04 Family case record

The product joins the shared record to the Life Plan, budget, and family evidence, then drafts the smallest complete packet for human review.

05 Outcome ledger

Submission, deficiency, revision, decision, and payment are recorded against the exact route, FI adapter, and evidence state.

The live product at opwddkit.com is the public surface of that system, not a generic provider list. A deterministic miner resolves a provider to its actual location, offering, and reimbursement path before that evidence is reusable in a family packet.

1,275 provider and program records ingested; 1,263 are currently published in the live, source-backed catalog
127 FI-scoped, quoted process claims from 51 reviewed documents across 25 Fiscal Intermediaries
122 executable statewide routing and evidence contracts across 85 expense and service families; 120 are atomically bound

How it gets better

Every FI has an adapter slot. When a published rule is captured it fills the exact form, timing, or payment step; when it is missing, Kit creates a confirmation job instead of copying another FI’s process. That makes coverage additive. With explicit consent, every returned packet, deficiency, revision, decision, and payment also becomes a calibration record for the exact route, FI adapter, and family fact pattern. Sparse or conflicting cohorts abstain; a learned pattern never silently rewrites a deterministic rule.

02

field signal → reliability layer

Local legal AI · Harvey LAB

An answer is not the product. The verification system is.

Kit led to a conversation with a small law firm that had banned AI firm-wide. Running a local model addressed their privacy concern, but not the one that mattered: a fluent answer can still miss an issue, cite the wrong provision, or turn uncertainty into confidence. I treated the legal answer as one artifact inside a system that has to show its work.

What I did not build

A nicer legal chatbot

That would have made a fluent answer easier to produce and a serious error harder to see. The more useful question was what has to be true before an AI answer enters a real workflow.

The product decision

Build the reliability layer around it

I mapped 25 practice areas into 1,196 workflow families and 1,749 task scenarios, then built a source-bounded pipeline for retrieval, reranking, evidence selection, and missing-issue checks. Every run locks the task, corpus, model, evidence, and score together so the next failure becomes a benchmark case.

Recall@5 moved from 84.6% to 100%. The stricter pairwise verifier selected 11 of 13 correct spans with zero false positives. A composed verifier selected all 13 on this development set. That is not a broad legal-AI claim. It shows the product shape: source retrieval, atomic checks, an issue ledger, and a preserved run record that can make the next evaluation or workflow better.

03

constraint → technical judgment

Itchy · OpenAI Parameter Golf

Build the benchmark before you believe the model.

The challenge was to build the strongest possible language model in a 16 MB artifact. The conventional answer was to shrink a token-based model. I started from the artifact constraint instead: use raw bytes, remove tokenizer overhead, and spend more of the fixed budget on the model. I built the model and the evaluation system together so there was a real comparison, not an architecture story.

The nontraditional bet

Build small from birth

I built the model, data pipeline, training loop, quantization path, local MLX implementation, CUDA path, and comparison harness. A 256-byte vocabulary avoided the usual tokenizer and embedding overhead, leaving more of the 16 MB budget for the network itself.

The product-quality gate

Invalidate the exciting result

An early patching run looked exceptional. I traced the data flow and found that 11 of every 12 target bytes were visible in the input. I invalidated the result, removed the leakage, and kept the clean rerun beside the original experiment instead of optimizing around a false win.

The byte-level model finished about 12% behind the token baseline, not ahead. That was closer than I expected from a fresh raw-byte pipeline operating on longer sequences, but it was not a win. The clean comparison made the next decision clear: focus on the infrastructure gap before claiming an architectural advantage. The leak stayed in the record too, because benchmark integrity was part of the product work.

04

family need → software factory

Kit Studio · Special interests and private games

Build the game the kid actually asked for.

Through my board work with the Simons Foundation, I kept hearing a small version of a hard problem from parents: there are no games for my kid. I started building private, parent-reviewed games around a child’s real interests, language, and sensory needs. It has been the most rewarding work I have done. The first vault holds 13 real games. What compounds is the software factory behind them.

The input is not a diagnosis

Start with the thing that already holds attention

A parent may not know which app their child needs. They do know what they return to, how they describe it, what overwhelms them, and what rules make play feel safe. That becomes a private profile and a build brief, not a generic personalization form.

The product decision

Build an interest-to-game production system

The shared layer resolves a child’s special interest into reusable patterns and game mechanics. The private layer adds their vocabulary, accessibility constraints, and parent rules. The factory then creates one self-contained game for parent review and a private vault, instead of turning children’s data into a public content feed.

The system

01Ask

A parent requests a free game in plain language, including the rules that matter.

02Resolve

The request maps to an interest record and the mechanics, sensory defaults, and language it suggests.

03Build

A constrained builder creates a touch-first game with no accounts, ads, fail states, or surprise stimulation.

04Approve

The parent sees it before the child does. The game belongs in that family’s private vault.

Annoyance Miner · Research infrastructure

A complaint is not an opportunity until it survives the evidence.

Product ideas too often start from a loud anecdote. I built an archive-first mining system to preserve the literal complaint, source, date, and context before anyone turns it into a solution. It separates repeated, fixable friction from a good story about a one-off annoyance.

Open for X · Replacement-app factory

Start with the bad experience, then make the smallest better product.

This began with a very dad-specific frustration: so many simple apps are built around ads, subscriptions, interruptions, and basic unreliability. Open for X is the public product layer on top of the miner. It takes recurring evidence, narrows it to a believable first version, and shows whether the work has actually reached TestFlight or a live release.

The system

Review evidence → deduplicated failure pattern → narrow replacement brief → build status → field signal for the next version.
