applied ai systems · research note · 2026

i build the systems that tell us when ai is wrong.

All three projects began with the same practical question: can sensitive work move to the model instead of sending sensitive data to the cloud? The first led me to train a tiny language model. The second showed me that local inference alone does not make an AI system trustworthy. The third put the same question in a domain that has no benchmark to borrow, so I had to build the measurement first. The common thread is measurement: making quality measurable, failures clear, and results honest enough to act on.

01

model training

itchy

what can a 16 MB model teach us about private, on-device ai?

I was working on a product whose core workflow analyzes extremely sensitive medical files. Keeping that analysis on the user's device, ideally an iPhone, would keep the files inside a clear privacy boundary. It would also make memory, speed, power use, and model quality core design constraints.

the test

OpenAI's Parameter Golf challenge gave me a fixed 16 MB limit for testing one part of that problem. My hypothesis was that a byte-level model could eliminate tokenizer and embedding overhead, then spend more of a fixed parameter budget on the network itself. This was not a clinical model or proof of iPhone viability. It was a focused test of whether byte-level input could improve quality under tight device limits.

what i built

A from-scratch language model, data pipeline, training loop, quantization path, local MLX implementation, CUDA training path, and a repeatable comparison harness. I trained and compared model variants rather than fine-tuning an existing foundation model.

the important failure

A patching experiment produced implausibly strong scores. I traced the data flow and found that 11 of every 12 target bytes were visible in the input. I invalidated the result publicly, removed the leak, and reran the same comparison fairly.

02

evaluation systems

local legal ai + harvey lab

how do you know when a legal ai system is wrong?

A small firm told me it was uncomfortable sending client material to cloud AI systems. I started with the same instinct: bring the model to the data. I ran Qwen3 locally on Apple Silicon and began adapting realistic legal tasks to local inference.

the more useful question

Running locally kept the data off the cloud, but did not give a lawyer a reason to trust the answer. The serious failures were often quieter than a made-up citation: a required issue left out, the wrong document treated as controlling, evidence overstated, or an uncertain record presented as a clean conclusion. Privacy got me started. Trust became the harder problem. That shifted the work from packaging local models to building the search, testing, and evidence checks around them.

03

building the benchmark

opwdd requirements engine

what do you do when the domain has no benchmark to borrow?

Applying for New York disability services means assembling an evidence packet. If one piece is missing or too old, the agency sends a letter asking for it, and the family loses months. I had access to a real eligibility case, including the agency's own correspondence, which meant I had something rare: a documented outcome I could test a system against.

the measurement problem

In the legal work I had 1,749 scored task scenarios to adapt. Benefits eligibility has no public benchmark, no leaderboard, and no agreed scoring rules. It also has a different failure mode. A fabricated citation gets caught by a caseworker. A quietly missing evaluation does not, and that is the failure that costs a family a year. So the first job was not building the system. It was deciding what would count as being right, and finding a test the system could not have been fitted to.

5official sources, 3 hash-pinned
11process stages modeled

what i built

A versioned rulebook compiled only from official agency publications, committed alongside the source PDFs and their SHA-256 hashes so anyone can confirm which bytes it was built from. The engine over it is pure functions: the language model extracts facts from documents, and never decides whether a requirement is met. Where the agency publishes a firm limit the rule is marked hard. Where it leaves a threshold to judgment, the rule is marked as my interpretation and the app says so. Corrections are append-only, so a person fixes a fact once and every dependent output re-derives.

what i measured

I froze the case record to its state on September 8, 2025, typed the evidence shapes by hand, and ran the engine with the clock set to that date. Then I compared its output to the agency's real additional-information letter, dated October 14, 2025. The rulebook was compiled from published guidance, not from the letter, so it could not have been fitted to the answer.

The engine named all three evidence gaps the letter went on to request, and flagged none of the three requirements the packet already satisfied. It is not true that it flagged only those three. It raised two more required items: specialty medical documentation, which the agency never asked for and which I count as a false positive, and a transmittal cover form, which is an artifact of my reconstruction because I typed in only the clinical documents. Both are pinned in a test so the claim cannot quietly drift. Two of the three hits also depend on recency thresholds the agency does not publish, which are marked as interpretation in the rulebook. That the letter agreed with them is evidence they are calibrated, not proof they are the agency's rule. And this is one case.

the second measurement

On the same corpus I built a retrieval and citation system over 13,061 passages, indexed locally for zero cost, where every answer carries verbatim source spans. Its own gold set scores 11 of 11 on answer accuracy and 3 of 3 on abstention, meaning it refuses to invent facts the record does not contain. Those numbers are development-set only and the gold set is private medical material, so I do not offer them as external evidence. The design claim is the part that generalizes: a system that can decline to answer is worth more than one that always will.

the honest boundary

The engine does not determine eligibility. The agency does. It recites the rule, cites the document, and routes every judgment that needs a credential to a person who has one. The backtest is engine-level, run on reconstructed evidence shapes rather than the original PDFs end to end. The published repository contains no personal, medical, or family records, and its fixtures are synthetic.

04

predictive evaluation

teek

how do you tell whether a model is reading someone or reciting a stereotype of them?

The legal and eligibility work both came down to the same question: is this output grounded in the record, or is it something that just sounds right? I started asking that question about a different kind of AI output: simulated judgment of a real person. Anyone can prompt "act like Warren Buffett" and get something Buffett-flavored back. In 1949 Bertram Forer handed students personality write-ups they rated 4.26 out of 5 for accuracy. Every student got the identical text, copied from an astrology column. Feeling right is worthless. I wanted a method where the profile could be wrong, and checked.

the landscape check

Before building, I checked who else was in this space. Simile raised a $100M Series A in February 2026, led by Index with Karpathy and Fei-Fei Li, founded by the lead author of the Stanford/DeepMind generative-agent-simulation paper, the only rigorous predictive-fidelity number in the category. No funded company in the landscape simulates a named living person without consent, and several 2026 statutes make that a deliberate line, not an oversight. That ruled out competing on a roster. It did not rule out publishing the measurement discipline the category is missing.

what i built

A falsifiable protocol: cut a subject's public corpus at a date, build the profile from only what came before, then score its predictions against specific decisions made after. Refusals are part of the method, not a gap in it. Big Five traits from text are out, because a meta-analysis across 85,724 people found linguistic methods explain 5.1% of the variance in self-reported personality. Leadership Trait Analysis is out, because its scores are only meaningful against proprietary norming distributions I don't have.

the blind-coding delta

A model coding a passage by a famous person may be reading the text, or retrieving what it absorbed about that person in training. Both look identical. So I code every passage twice: once with the speaker named, once with identifiers stripped, and measure the gap. Small gap, it's reading. Large gap, it's reciting a stereotype and the profile is circular. As far as the landscape research found, nobody else in this category measures that.

the shared pattern

models improve when the data, evaluation, and feedback loop become a system.

01define the task

Turn a real workflow into clear success and failure.

02lock the test

Keep development data, evidence, scoring rules, and final tests separate.

03measure failures

Separate search, evidence, missing-issue, model, output, and code errors.

04improve the system

Change data, retrieval, routing, verification, or the model only when evidence supports it.

the honest scope

I have trained models from scratch in Itchy. In the legal and eligibility work, I did not fine-tune a model. I built and tested the surrounding ML system: local inference, benchmark adaptation, retrieval, evals, verification, source tracking, and repeatable tests. The four cases sit at different points on one axis. Itchy used a benchmark someone else defined. The legal work adapted an existing benchmark to a new setting. The eligibility work had no benchmark to adapt, so the deliverable was the measurement itself. Teek goes one step further: the measurement itself needed a check for whether the model was reasoning or reciting, so that became part of the deliverable too. That is the work I would bring to an applied AI or forward-deployed team, alongside experience building and leading technical organizations.

The working pattern across all four projects is simple: start with a blocked customer workflow, isolate the real technical constraint, build enough of the stack to test it, discard the attractive answer when the evidence disagrees, and leave behind a reusable quality loop. The clearest signal in all of it is what each project reports when the result is unflattering: a leaked benchmark invalidated, a perfect score labeled as development-set only, two extra flags disclosed instead of rounded away.
