00 / paul jump
new york / 2026

builder + technical leader

i build to find out what deserves to exist.

after ten years at uber, ai made trying cheap. i built products for cities, renters, dates, kids, lawyers, and people trying to understand themselves.

most weren't companies. every one left behind code, data, or a better question. the useful parts became the next build.

01 / thesis

the short version

the model isn't the product.

the product is the context you collect, the workflow you finish, and the proof that the result holds up.

02 / selected work

seven builds

the same problem kept showing up in different forms.

01

colosseum

generation got cheap. judgment didn't.

one model can make a confident answer feel finished. i built a debate arena that makes several models argue, show their sources, and face a separate judge.

// what stayed

the useful product is the system that challenges the answer and leaves receipts.

open colosseum ↗
02

unignorable

the city closes tickets. people live with what comes back.

problems around my kids' school kept getting reported to 311, closed, and reported again. i joined 455,000 reports into one history and mapped what the city kept forgetting.

// what stayed

the person facing an institution needs memory stronger than the institution's workflow.

open unignorable ↗
03

pricefixed

sometimes the missing product is a missing data layer.

the facts a renter needs are split across listings, public records, price histories, and landlord portfolios. i joined them across millions of apartments.

// what stayed

consumer power starts by rebuilding the context that institutions already have.

view the open tools ↗
04

unhinged

personal software doesn't have to be extractive.

i wanted to know whether an iphone and apple watch could tell you what your body already knew about a date. the result was a private report built from 16 on-device signals.

// what stayed

useful ai can stay personal without turning a life into somebody else's dataset.

open unhinged ↗
05

rexair + walt's workshop

software for one person is a real market now.

my sons needed software built around how they actually think. i built rex a real-flight learning system and gave walt a voice-to-game loop that shipped 13 games from his own ideas.

// what stayed

start with one person. keep the pattern that works for the next family.

open rexair ↗open walt's workshop ↗
06

slopify

you can own more of the stack than you think.

a joke about spotify deleting ai music became a streaming product, a locally generated catalog, and an iphone app that makes music without a generation api.

// what stayed

local models change which products can exist and who gets to control them.

open slopify ↗
07

teek + vibecheck + whetstone

sounding right isn't the same as being right.

i built source-backed models of how people decide, a private profiler of how i build, and evals that force ai systems to show where their claims came from.

// what stayed

in consequential workflows, provenance and evaluation are the product.
