A thesis, and a working proof

The constraint is not the model. It is the ground truth.

Every AI feature shipped into a regulated workflow adds surface area for being confidently wrong. What caps that is not how smart the model gets. It is how fast the company can manufacture truth it is willing to stand behind.

Paul Jump · outside view, built from public sources · use ↓ or → to move

Start here

The product was never the software.

People do not buy tax software. They buy a sentence: someone will stand behind this number.

Accuracy guarantees. Audit support. Maximum refund promises. Those are not marketing wrappers around the product — they are the product. The software is the delivery mechanism for a liability someone else agreed to carry.

What a customer is actually buying

Permission to stop worrying about a decision they cannot personally verify.

What that makes the asset

Not the interface. The willingness — and the ability — to be accountable for an answer.

The asymmetry nobody prices correctly

99% right is not worth 99%.

For drafting an email, a 1% error rate is a rounding error — you can see the mistake and fix it in two seconds.

For a tax position, a benefits claim, or a filing deadline, the user cannot tell which 1% they are in. So the value of the whole system collapses toward the value of its worst answer. Trust is not linear in accuracy.

This is why the same model that is transformative in a writing tool is a liability in a compliance workflow, with no change to the model at all.

recoverable

Wrong draft email

Visible, instant, free to fix.

not recoverable

Wrong filing position

Invisible at the moment of harm. Surfaces months later, addressed to the customer, in an envelope with a deadline on it.

The reframe

Model capability is improving on somebody else's roadmap and somebody else's budget. Ground truth about what the rules are, when they changed, and which ones died is not.

Frontier capability is a rising tide that lifts every competitor at the same time. It is not a moat and it was never going to be one.

A structured, citable, drift-watched body of truth about a specific regulated domain is the only part of the stack that is durably yours — because nobody else is building yours, and it compounds every time a rule moves.

  • Bought, not built — model weights, context length, inference cost
  • Built, and only by you — what the rule is, what it means for this customer, what changed last quarter, and what you are willing to say out loud

An outsider's hypothesis — stated as a question, not a claim

Where does truth live today, and can it feed an agent?

In most companies of this shape, the answer to "what is the rule?" lives in three places, none of which an agent can query:

place 1

Content

Prose written for a human reader. Authoritative, and structurally unusable as a machine precondition.

place 2

Expert heads

The most valuable version, and the only one that never gets written down. It walks out at 5pm and retires eventually.

place 3

Code

Precise, and silent about its own provenance. It can tell you the answer but not why, or when it stopped being right.

I am not claiming to know your internals. I am claiming this is the default shape everywhere, and that it is the actual thing standing between an AI roadmap and a shippable one. If it is already solved, that is the first thing I would want to be wrong about.

What "ground truth as an asset" actually means

Content is written to be read. Truth has to be written to be checked.

Same subject matter, completely different object. Every claim carries five things, or the system cannot safely act on it:

01

The atomic claim

One checkable statement. Not a paragraph.

02

The verbatim quote and its source

So a human can go read the sentence you relied on.

03

The tier

Did we read the issuing authority, or are we summarizing someone who did?

04

The effective date

Rules are true for a period, not forever.

05

Its life status

Live, superseded, or struck down — never deleted.

Then, and only then

A routing contract can bind that claim to a decision and an agent can be allowed to act.

The sharpest failure, and the least discussed

A corpus that deletes dead rules cannot tell you it was wrong.

If you remove a rule when it dies, you destroy the difference between we never knew about this and we knew, and it changed.

That is the exact mechanism by which a system becomes confidently wrong. The dead rule leaves no scar, so nothing downstream knows to stop citing it.

In the system on the last slide, one federal rule in the corpus is a court-vacated 2025 regulation. It is retained, tagged, and the generated letter says in its own body that it does not rely on it. That is not a detail. That is the design.

deletes

Confidently wrong

Generates an authoritative letter resting on a rule that no longer exists. No signal anywhere that anything is off.

retains and tags

Wrong out loud

Knows the rule died, knows the date, and says so in the artifact it hands the customer.

The move

Abstention is not governance. It is a product surface.

The industry treats "knowing when not to answer" as a policy problem — guardrails, evals, human-in-the-loop. Those are review processes. They never reach the customer. The refusals are the most trust-generating thing the system does, so they should be the most visible. Four kinds, each a screen:

"This protection does not reach your coverage."

Scope. Told in ten seconds instead of discovered after two phone calls.

"That rule was struck down in July 2025, so this does not rely on it."

Currency. The system knows what it used to believe.

"We could not read the agency's own page, so this is a summary, not a quote."

Provenance. It distinguishes what it verified from what it inherited.

"That 120-day window binds the hospital, not you."

Direction. A clock restraining the institution is not a deadline you can miss.

Proof

So I built one, deliberately in a domain you do not operate in.

If I had built this in tax, we would spend this meeting arguing about my tax content, and the architecture would never come up.

The domain is medical bills — federal protections that erase parts of what people are billed. Fourteen of them, each with cited claims, hard clocks, and a generated letter. The domain is the disposable part. The corpus shape, the routing contracts, and the refusals are what transfer.

What is real

The rules, the citations, the routing, the letters, the refusals, the clocks.

What is not

Users. One domain, one person, no traction. It is an existence proof, not a business.

The five layers — identical in every domain

Only the contents change.

01

Rule contract

What it covers, what defeats it, what the clock is, what bounces a first attempt.

02

Institution adapter

Their form, channel and timing — in its own slot, because their process is not the law.

03

Evidence layer

Cited, quoted, tiered, dated. Dead rules retained and labelled.

04

Packet and review

Smallest complete artifact. Every human-issued blocker named. The human signs.

05

Outcome ledger

Sent, bounced, decided — recorded against the exact rule version that produced it.

Layer five is why this is a platform and not a services motion. The first case is bespoke. The second inherits everything the first one paid for. Headcount grows by category, not by customer.

Where this lands — in priority order

Three places, and only the third is a new product.

platform

1 · The trust substrate

A tiered, effective-dated, citable rules layer with explicit abstention contracts, sitting under the assistant rather than beside it.

Serves every bet at once. Nothing customer-facing has to change to start.

cheapest

2 · The expert flywheel

Every correction a live expert makes to an AI-prepared draft is a labeled training example. The company already pays for the labor and mostly discards the label.

Entirely internal. No new customer surface, no regulatory exposure.

provocative

3 · Score → advocacy

A consumer money surface with well over a hundred million members currently shows you a number and sells you a product. It could instead go get your money back.

Medical collections is the wedge because it is already on the report being shown. Same object, not an adjacency leap.

Sequenced deliberately: the first two are capability work with no launch risk. The third is a bet, and it should not be taken until the first two have made it cheap.

Discipline

What I am not proposing.

  • Not a health product. This is a credit-report and money-recovery motion that happens to touch medical bills. Nobody is entering healthcare.
  • Not a membership business. The demo carries a membership frame because it was built standalone. Inside a company with an existing consumer surface, that frame is wrong and should be dropped.
  • Not a services organization. If layer five does not exist, this is consulting and it should be killed. Layer five is the whole argument.
  • Not a new brand. Everything here is a capability under an existing surface.

A strategy that cannot say what it excludes is not a strategy. Each of these was a real temptation while building, and each is a way this becomes off-mission within two quarters.

How you would know it is working

Measure the refusals, not the answers.

Abstention rate, held deliberately above zero

A system that never declines is not calibrated, it is just confident. Falling to zero is the alarm.

Expert edit distance, trending down

How much a human changes before signing. The single cleanest quality signal, and it is free to collect.

First-attempt acceptance

Did the artifact survive the institution's review without a deficiency round.

Cost of the second domain

If domain two does not cost meaningfully less than domain one, layer five is not real and the platform claim is false.

The ask

One workflow, ninety days, through the experts you already have.

Pick one rules-heavy workflow that is under-owned today. Build the five layers for it. Route it through existing human experts so accountability never moves.

Deliver three things: the working layer, a reliability harness with an explicit do-not-ship threshold, and a written spec for which pieces graduate to platform.

Then a second, unrelated workflow on the same spine. One workflow is a feature. Two unrelated ones on shared infrastructure is a factory.

What success looks like

Domain two is visibly cheaper than domain one, and you can point at the parts that made it cheaper.

What failure looks like

Domain two costs the same. Then this was bespoke work wearing a platform costume, and the honest move is to stop.
